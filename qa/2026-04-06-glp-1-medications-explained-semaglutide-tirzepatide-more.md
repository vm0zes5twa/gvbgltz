What Are GLP-1 Medications?

GLP-1 stands for glucagon-like peptide-1, a naturally occurring hormone your body produces in the small intestine released after eating. This hormone plays a key role in controlling blood sugar, slowing digestion, and helping you feel full after eating.

GLP-1 medications are synthetic versions (analogs) of this hormone. When injected into the body, they mimic and enhance the hormone’s natural functions. This has a profound effect on both blood sugar control and appetite regulation, which often results in weight loss. (1)

Originally approved to treat type 2 diabetes, researchers began creating more targeted versions of GLP-1 medications after it was found that diabetes patients taking the medication often lost weight, sometimes without even intentionally trying. (2) In fact, studies have shown that these medications can often have a staggering impact, with large-scale studies showing that patients using semaglutide lost nearly 15 percent of body weight over 68 weeks, compared to just under a 3 percent loss for those using a placebo. (3) In the SURMOUNT-4 trial, patients with obesity receiving tirzepatide had a mean 25.3% body weight loss over 88 weeks. More than half of the participants receiving tirzepatide lost 25% or more of their body weight during the trial period. (4)

How Do GLP-1s Work in the Body?

GLP-1 medications work on multiple systems in the body, which is why they produce such wide-ranging effects. These medications don’t simply work as appetite suppressants. Instead, they can help rebalance core hormonal pathways involved in metabolism and energy regulation if used properly with proper clinical oversight.

GLP-1 medications work by:

Stimulating insulin secretion in response to food, helping move sugar from your blood into your cells more efficiently.

Suppresses glucagon production, which lowers blood sugar levels between meals.

Delaying gastric emptying, meaning food stays in your stomach longer—often leading to increased fullness and reduced hunger.

Acting on GLP-1 receptors in the brain, directly influencing appetite centers and potentially reducing cravings.

Supporting metabolic balance, making it easier to burn fat and prevent new fat accumulation. (1)

This multi-layered mechanism helps explain why GLP-1s are being used beyond people with diabetes. They can also have powerful effects on controlling hunger, shaping body composition, and stabilizing weight management.

Side Effects and Safety of GLP-1 Therapy

While most men tolerate GLP-1 medications well, especially when the dose is increased gradually, it’s important to note that all patients will react differently to new medication. A slow ramp-up of GLP-1 therapy allows the body to adjust and reduces the likelihood of side effects.

The most common GLP-1 side effects include mild to moderate nausea, diarrhea, occasional vomiting or upset stomach, constipation, stomach pain, temporary fatigue, increased heart rate, and headaches. (10)

In most cases, these symptoms improve after the first few weeks of treatment. Eating smaller meals and avoiding high-fat foods during the adjustment period can also help.

Serious side effects are rare, but may include gallbladder inflammation or gallstones, pancreatitis, or kidney function issues. Regular check-ins with your provider, routine lab work, and close monitoring are crucial to ensuring any side effects are being diagnosed and treated efficiently.

# Abdominal Pain: When to Worry? 7 Red Flags to Act Now | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 1/29/2026

When should I worry about abdominal pain?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Abdominal pain requires urgent medical care if it is sudden, severe, worsening, or lasts more than 2–3 days. Red-flag symptoms that demand immediate attention include fever, persistent vomiting, blood in stool or vomit, chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, or a rigid, tender belly. Never ignore abdominal pain during pregnancy.

You should also consult a doctor if pain recurs, interferes with daily life, or is paired with unexplained weight loss or significant bowel changes. Children and older adults need extra attention, as they often display fewer obvious symptoms. Location of the pain and accompanying warning signs are key to identifying the cause and determining next steps.

Abdominal pain is one of the most common reasons people seek medical advice. It can range from a mild, short‑lived ache to severe pain that needs urgent care. Most abdominal pain is not dangerous and improves on its own, but some patterns and symptoms should not be ignored.

This guide explains when you should worry about abdominal pain, how to recognize warning signs, and when it's time to speak to a doctor. The goal is to help you make informed decisions without unnecessary fear.

Understanding abdominal pain

Abdominal pain is discomfort felt anywhere between the chest and the groin. It may feel:

Sharp or dull

Crampy or constant

Burning, bloated, or pressure‑like

The cause can be something minor, such as indigestion or gas, or something more serious, such as an infection, inflammation, or blockage.

Doctors often assess abdominal pain based on:

Location (upper, lower, right, left, or central)

Severity

Timing (sudden or gradual)

Associated symptoms

Your age and medical history

Common, usually harmless causes of abdominal pain

Many cases of abdominal pain are uncomfortable but not dangerous. Examples include:

Indigestion or acid reflux

Gas or bloating

Constipation

Mild food poisoning

Stomach flu (viral gastroenteritis)

Menstrual cramps

Muscle strain from exercise or coughing

This type of abdominal pain often improves within hours to a few days and responds to rest, fluids, or over‑the‑counter remedies.

When abdominal pain becomes a concern

You should start paying closer attention if abdominal pain:

Lasts longer than a few days

Keeps coming back

Is getting worse instead of better

Interferes with eating, sleeping, or daily activities

While these signs don't automatically mean something serious, they do suggest it's time to check in with a healthcare professional.

Red flags: when to worry about abdominal pain

Certain symptoms combined with abdominal pain can signal a more serious problem. Seek medical care promptly if you notice any of the following.

1. Severe or sudden abdominal pain

Pain that starts suddenly and feels intense

Pain described as the "worst ever"

Pain that makes it hard to stand, walk, or find a comfortable position

Sudden severe abdominal pain can be linked to conditions such as appendicitis, gallbladder problems, kidney stones, or reduced blood flow to the intestines.

2. Abdominal pain with fever

Fever along with abdominal pain

Chills or shaking

Feeling generally unwell

This combination may suggest an infection or inflammation, such as appendicitis, diverticulitis, or an abdominal abscess.

3. Abdominal pain with vomiting or persistent nausea

Green, yellow, or bloody

Accompanied by dehydration (dry mouth, little urine, dizziness)

These symptoms may point to a bowel obstruction, infection, or pancreatitis.

4. Blood in stool or vomit

Black, tar‑like stools

Bright red blood in stool

Vomiting blood or material that looks like coffee grounds

These signs can indicate bleeding in the digestive tract and should always be evaluated urgently.

5. Abdominal pain with chest pain or shortness of breath

Sometimes pain from the heart or lungs can be felt in the upper abdomen. Seek urgent care if abdominal pain occurs with:

Chest pressure or tightness

Shortness of breath

Sweating or nausea

This is especially important for older adults or people with heart disease risk factors.

6. Abdominal pain during pregnancy

Abdominal pain in pregnancy should never be ignored, especially if it is:

Severe or one‑sided

Accompanied by vaginal bleeding

Associated with dizziness or fainting

These symptoms may suggest ectopic pregnancy, miscarriage, or other pregnancy‑related complications.

7. Abdominal pain in children or older adults

Young children may not be able to explain pain clearly

Older adults may have serious conditions with mild symptoms

In both groups, abdominal pain should be taken seriously and assessed sooner rather than later.

Location‑based abdominal pain concerns

Where the pain is felt can provide important clues.

Right lower abdomen: Possible appendicitis

Right upper abdomen: Gallbladder or liver issues

Upper middle abdomen: Ulcers, gastritis, or pancreatitis

Lower abdomen: Urinary tract infections, bowel issues, or reproductive organ problems

Pain that moves, especially from the center to the lower right abdomen, deserves prompt evaluation.

Chronic abdominal pain: when ongoing pain matters

Chronic abdominal pain (lasting weeks or months) is rarely an emergency, but it should still be evaluated. Possible causes include:

Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)

Food intolerances

Chronic constipation

Ulcers

While these conditions are often manageable, ongoing abdominal pain should not be ignored, especially if it affects quality of life.

When to seek urgent medical care

Seek immediate medical help or emergency care if abdominal pain is accompanied by:

Severe, sudden pain

Fainting or confusion

Signs of internal bleeding

A rigid, hard, or very tender abdomen

High fever with worsening pain

These symptoms can be life‑threatening and require urgent evaluation.

Key takeaways

Most abdominal pain is not serious, but patterns matter

Sudden, severe, or worsening pain deserves attention

Certain symptoms, like fever, vomiting, or bleeding, are warning signs

Trust your instincts—if something feels wrong, get it checked

Always speak to a doctor about symptoms that could be serious or life‑threatening

Abdominal pain is common, but ignoring important signs can delay care. Staying informed and acting early when needed can make a meaningful difference in your health.

How Oral Hydration Works

When you drink water, it travels through the digestive tract—starting in your mouth, moving to your stomach, and then into the intestines, where it’s absorbed into the bloodstream. This process helps maintain fluid balance and supports the body’s daily hydration needs.

Oral hydration is a slow and steady process. It works best when consumed consistently throughout the day. However, there are limitations. During illness (e.g., vomiting, diarrhea) or after strenuous physical activity, your body may need faster or more targeted hydration than your digestive system can provide. Excessive sweating, heat exposure, and travel can also deplete fluids and electrolytes more quickly than water alone can replenish.

How IV Therapy Works

IV therapy bypasses the digestive system entirely. Instead, fluids are infused directly into the bloodstream through a vein delivering hydration, electrolytes, and often vitamins or medications immediately and in precise quantities.

This method is commonly used in hospitals to treat dehydration caused by illness, surgery, or trauma. In recent years, it has also gained popularity in wellness and recovery services for those who want a fast and efficient way to rehydrate.

Rapid rehydration: Immediate delivery of fluids helps restore hydration faster than oral intake.

How Much Water is Equivalent to an IV?

A typical IV therapy session delivers between 500 mL to 1,000 mL (1–2 liters) of fluid directly into the bloodstream. Because oral absorption is less efficient, it might take 2–3 liters (or 8–12 cups) of water to match the hydration benefits of a single IV drip. That doesn’t even account for added electrolytes or nutrients that IV therapy may include.

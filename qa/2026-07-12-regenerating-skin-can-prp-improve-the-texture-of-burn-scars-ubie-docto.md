Explanation

Burn scars often leave behind changes in texture, color and pliability that can impact both appearance and comfort. Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) has emerged as a promising option in dermatology and plastic surgery for enhancing tissue repair. In this article, we'll explore how PRP for burn scars works, what the evidence shows, and practical considerations if you're thinking about this treatment.

What Is PRP and Why It Matters for Burn Scars

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) is a concentration of your own blood's platelets suspended in a small volume of plasma. Platelets carry growth factors and cytokines that help orchestrate wound healing. When injected or applied topically, PRP can:

Stimulate collagen and elastin production

Promote new blood vessel formation (angiogenesis)

Modulate inflammation

Enhance tissue remodeling

For burn scars—where normal healing was disrupted—these processes may help soften scar tissue, smooth out irregularities and improve skin quality over time.

How PRP for Burn Scars Works

Blood draw and centrifugation• A small sample of your blood is spun to separate red cells from plasma.• The goal is to isolate a high-platelet fraction.

Activation (optional)• Calcium or thrombin may be added to trigger platelet degranulation.• This releases growth factors immediately.

Application or injection• PRP can be injected into deep scar tissue to remodel collagen.• It may also be applied topically after microneedling to enhance penetration.

Follow-up sessions• Multiple treatments, spaced 4–6 weeks apart, tend to yield better results.

In a 2020 case series, patients reported softer, more supple scars after combined microneedling and PRP sessions, with objective improvements on ultrasound skin imaging.

A randomized controlled trial in an aesthetic surgery journal compared PRP plus silicone gel versus silicone gel alone; the PRP group showed significantly better color uniformity and pliability at 12 weeks.

Benefits of PRP for Burn Scars

Supports natural, autologous (self-derived) healing

May reduce reliance on steroids or other medications

Low risk of allergic reaction since it's your own blood

Can be combined safely with other scar-modulating therapies

Potential Risks and Limitations

Mild discomfort or bruising at the injection site

Risk of infection if strict sterile technique isn't followed

Not a quick "fix"—multiple treatments are often needed

Results can be subtle; PRP won't erase scars completely

Cost varies widely and is often not covered by insurance

Combining PRP With Other Treatments

PRP for burn scars often works best as part of a multimodal approach:

Microneedling: Creates micro-channels for PRP to penetrate deeper

Fractional laser resurfacing: Addresses pigmentation and uneven texture

Silicone sheets or gels: Offer continued scar hydration and pressure

Practical Considerations

Choosing a qualified provider• Look for board-certified dermatologists or plastic surgeons experienced in PRP techniques.

Pre-procedure assessment• Discuss any clotting disorders or medications (e.g., blood thinners).

Realistic expectations• PRP is complementary, not curative. It can improve texture and comfort but won't make scars vanish.

Cost and scheduling• Ask about package pricing for multiple sessions and follow-up visits.

When to Seek Professional Advice

Even with promising therapies like PRP, it's important to monitor your scars and overall health. If you experience:

Increasing pain, redness or swelling around the scar

Signs of infection (fever, warmth, pus)

Changes that concern you, such as rapid scar growth

The Bottom Line

PRP for burn scars leverages your body's own healing machinery to enhance texture, reduce thickness and improve scar flexibility. While research supports its use, remember that outcomes vary and multiple sessions are usually needed. Discuss your individual case with a qualified specialist to determine if PRP fits your scar-management plan.

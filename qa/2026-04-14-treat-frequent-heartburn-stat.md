# Treat Frequent Heartburn—Stat

[Heartburn Stat](https://barrierwatch.bbsxyxy.com/blog/0229829466.html)

It’s a night-out buzzkill akin to your team losing big. You go out for a great meal, have a few beers, and are feeling good, until, suddenly you aren’t. You’ve got heartburn with its tell-tale burning sensation that spreads up through the chest, then into the back of your throat. Often, heartburn can be super painful. And if it happens regularly, it can negatively impact your life, including how you sleep. “Frequent heartburn is when you experience symptoms two or more times a week,” says Clenton Coleman, MD, chief of internal medicine at Holy Name Hospital in New Jersey. Not only is that uncomfortable, it can be stress-inducing, which in turn can also exacerbate symptoms.

So what’s actually going on? “When you swallow food, it goes through the esophagus to something called your lower esophageal sphincter,” says David Chua, MD, a gastroenterologist with Summit Digestive in the Chicago area. “That opens up to let food into your stomach, then closes back up while your stomach is digesting,” he explains. “But if the sphincter isn’t working right and opens up when it’s not supposed to, the contents of your stomach can come back up, which can cause symptoms.” Adds Dr. Coleman, “You have a higher risk for it if you’re overweight or obese, have lots of stress, have poor sleep, or tend to overeat.”

It is possible to help prevent your stomach’s acid from going where it doesn’t belong. Try these tips to stop heartburn before it ever starts.

Identify trigger foods

An easy way to diminish you chances of heartburn is to not eat the things that bring it on. “Typical foods that can bring on heartburn are spicy, fatty, and fried,” explains Dr. Chau. Onions, citrus, tomatoes, and chocolate can also irritate, he adds. The triggers are different for everyone, so take some notes or keep a food diary so you can avoid aggravating foods.”

Prevent the Burn, Before it Starts

Nexium 24HR

While some heartburn meds are meant to be taken after the pain starts, others, like Nexium 24HR, are designed to help relieve frequent heartburn symptoms from occurring in the first place. Nexium 24HR works all day and all night to help prevent heartburn, notes Dr. Coleman. It’s recommended to take a pill in the morning before you start your daily routine.

Note: While occasional heartburn is relatively harmless, having it too frequently can cause potentially dangerous complications like esophagitis. Have chronic heartburn that just won’t go away? Talk to your doctor.

Eat early in the evening

The timing of meals can matter just as much as what you eat—especially late in the day. “You want to give your stomach time to digest before you lay down,” says Dr. Chua. Gravity ends up working against you when you’re lying flat, which can cause acid to flow in the opposite direction. According to research, it’s best to wrap up your nighttime noshing three hours before bedtime.

Move your body

There’s something to taking a post-meal stroll. “Exercise is very helpful since it helps digestion,” says Dr. Coleman. The movement increases blood flow, which in turn helps shuttle food through the system. “The less time your food is in your stomach, the less acid is made,” he adds. And when there’s less acid in your stomach, there’s less that can work its way back up your esophagus.

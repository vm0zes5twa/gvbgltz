Mitchell’s Skims collab reflects a years-long saga for Cleveland to return to its winning ways. Before Mitchell was traded to the Cavs in late 2022, Cleveland was not an encouraging place to be a basketball fan. It had been over four years since LeBron James left the city (for the second time) to take his talents to Los Angeles. The Cavs missed the playoffs every season following, and they also had to watch The King raise another banner with the team he left them for (again). In short, Cleveland lost the breakup and was stuck watching stories of their ex’s honeymoon pics on Instagram. Then, Mitchell came to town.

In his first season with the team, he led them to 51 wins and a playoff birth. In his second, a playoff series win. Now, three seasons in, the Cavs finished with the best record in the East and accomplished a few milestones not even LeBron had hit. For the first time in the history of the franchise, the team enjoyed a 16-game winning streak on its way to finishing the season with the second greatest offensive rating (a stat that measures a team's offensive efficiency by counting the number of points a team scores per 100 possessions) for any team in the history of the NBA.

For a team that drafted the greatest player of the last twenty years (and possibly ever), to be putting up historic numbers like this for the first time in franchise history is a monumental feat. But, to be fair, Mitchell and his Cavs had something LeBron never did—comfy, versatile underwear. “We go from shootaround to tip-off to team travel, and having [underwear] that works through it all just simplifies things,” he says. “Skims fits into every part of the day, and that kind of consistency helps keep my focus where it needs to be—on winning.”

This season, Donovan Mitchell has seemingly grabbed the title from LeBron James as the next great Cleveland Cavalier. But this generational passing of the baton has deeper roots than many realize. Despite being a native New Yorker, he grew up loving the Cavs, specifically LeBron, and was even in attendance when James announced he was leaving Cleveland for the first time in 2010 to go to Miami (where he won two championships). “Full circle. I was that kid watching history unfold, and now I’ve got a chance to write a new chapter for this city,” Mitchell says.

So what would it mean to bring a title back to Cleveland, the first ever without James, this season? “Bringing a title back to Cleveland would mean everything,” he says. “This city shows up. They deserve that moment, and we’re grinding to make it happen.”

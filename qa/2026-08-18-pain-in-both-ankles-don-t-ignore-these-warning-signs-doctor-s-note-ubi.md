Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Bilateral ankle pain—when both ankles hurt at the same time—is often linked to systemic conditions rather than a single injury. Common causes include bone marrow edema syndrome, which involves fluid buildup within the bone, and sarcoidosis, an inflammatory disease that can affect the joints. Other possibilities include rheumatoid arthritis, tendonitis, or overuse.

Explanation

Pain in both ankles simultaneously can be due to several conditions. One possible cause is Bone Marrow Edema Syndrome. This condition affects the bones in the foot and ankle, causing pain and swelling. It is not always clear why this happens, but it can make walking difficult.

Another possible cause is sarcoidosis, which is a disease that can affect many parts of the body, including the lungs and skin. Sometimes, sarcoidosis can cause pain in both ankles. This happens because the disease can lead to inflammation in the joints.

If you have pain in both ankles, it is important to talk to a healthcare provider.

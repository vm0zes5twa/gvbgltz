Persistent light-headedness and fatigue are common symptoms that can point to several underlying health conditions. The most frequent causes include:

Gastroparesis: A digestive disorder that slows stomach emptying, often leading to dizziness and low energy.

Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV): An inner ear issue that triggers brief spells of light-headedness with head movement.

Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD): A liver condition linked to fatigue and general weakness.

Other possibilities include anemia, dehydration, low blood sugar, thyroid problems, or medication side effects. Because these symptoms overlap across many conditions, identifying the true cause requires a careful review of your full symptom picture.

Explanation

Feeling light-headed and tired constantly can be linked to several medical conditions. Gastroparesis is one condition where the stomach takes too long to empty food. This can make you feel very tired. Another condition is benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV). BPPV can cause dizziness, and it's common to feel tired when this happens. Lastly, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) can also make you feel tired. This is when fat builds up in the liver, even if you don't drink much alcohol.

Conditions to Consider:

Gastroparesis: This condition affects how the stomach moves food. When the stomach doesn't work right, you might feel very tired.

BPPV: This is a problem with your inner ear that causes dizziness. Feeling dizzy can make you feel tired more often.

Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease: This is when fat builds up in the liver. It can make you feel tired because your liver isn't working as well as it should.

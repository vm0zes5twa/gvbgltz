Internal Medicine

Lyme disease is a tick-borne illness that can trigger a wide range of symptoms, including chronic fatigue and vertigo. When the infection spreads to the brain and nervous system—a condition known as neurological Lyme disease or neuroborreliosis—these symptoms often become more pronounced and persistent. Fatigue may feel debilitating, while vertigo can cause dizziness, balance issues, and disorientation.

Because Lyme symptoms overlap with many other conditions, identifying the cause early is critical for effective treatment. Explanation

Lyme disease is an infection that can sometimes affect the brain, leading to symptoms such as chronic fatigue and vertigo. When Lyme disease impacts the brain, it is known as Lyme encephalitis. This condition can cause a variety of symptoms, including tiredness that doesn't go away and feeling dizzy or off-balance, which is known as vertigo.

Chronic Fatigue: This means feeling very tired all the time, even after resting. It can make it hard to do everyday things.

Vertigo: This is when you feel like you're spinning or things around you are moving when they're not. It can make you feel dizzy and unsteady.

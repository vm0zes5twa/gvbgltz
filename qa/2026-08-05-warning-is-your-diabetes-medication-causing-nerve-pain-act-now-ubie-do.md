Explanation

If you're living with type 2 diabetes, you've likely heard about metformin—one of the most commonly prescribed medications for blood sugar control. While metformin is effective and generally well tolerated, long-term use can interfere with vitamin B12 absorption. Over time, low B12 levels can lead to nerve damage (neuropathy) and painful sensations in your hands and feet.

How Metformin Can Affect Your B12 Levels

Metformin helps lower blood sugar by improving insulin sensitivity and reducing glucose production in the liver. However, studies show that up to 30% of people on long-term metformin develop lower-than-normal B12 levels. Here's why:

Altered gut absorptionMetformin may change the bacteria and pH in your small intestine, reducing the absorption of the "intrinsic factor" that helps your body take in B12 from food.

Bile salt changesMetformin can affect how bile salts work, and these salts play a role in moving B12 from your intestines into your bloodstream.

Duration and dosageThe risk grows the longer you've been on metformin and with higher daily doses.

Because vitamin B12 is crucial for nerve health, a deficiency can mimic or worsen the nerve pain often attributed solely to diabetic neuropathy.

Signs of B12 Deficiency & Nerve Pain

Low B12 levels can cause a variety of symptoms that overlap with diabetic nerve damage. Pay attention if you notice any of the following:

Nerve-Related Symptoms

Numbness or reduced sensation

Muscle weakness or balance issues

Often these sensations start in the toes and fingers and progress upward. Left untreated, severe B12 deficiency can lead to irreversible nerve damage.

Managing and Preventing Nerve Pain

Once you know metformin is affecting your B12 levels, you can take steps to protect your nerves and reduce pain.

1. B12 Supplementation

Oral supplements: Daily doses (500–1,000 µg) can restore levels over months.

Injectable B12: For severe cases, your doctor may recommend intramuscular shots (1,000 µg every week until levels normalize, then monthly).

2. Dietary Sources of B12

Include B12-rich foods in your meals:

Lean meats (beef, pork, poultry)

Fish and shellfish (salmon, tuna, clams)

Dairy (milk, cheese, yogurt)

Fortified cereals and nutritional yeast

3. Review Your Diabetes Medications

In some cases, your doctor may:

Adjust your metformin dose

Add or switch to another diabetes medication (e.g., SGLT-2 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists)

Monitor blood sugar more closely to safely lower metformin

Tips to Lower Your Risk

Have your B12 level checked at least once a year if you're on metformin.

Discuss preventive B12 supplementation with your healthcare provider.

Maintain a balanced diet with adequate B12.

Keep up routine diabetes check-ups and blood tests.

Early detection and action make a significant difference in preventing long-term nerve damage.

When to Speak to Your Doctor

Nerve pain can hurt your daily life and signal more serious issues. Contact your doctor if you experience:

New or worsening tingling, burning, or numbness

Muscle weakness or difficulty walking

Severe fatigue, dizziness, or mood changes

Any sign of infection or non-healing wound on your feet

Always consult a medical professional about any life-threatening or serious symptoms.

Swollen lymph nodes on one side of the neck are most often caused by localized infections. Common culprits include tonsillitis, submandibular gland issues (such as salivary stones or infection), and tick-borne illnesses like Lyme disease. Less commonly, one-sided neck swelling may signal a dental infection, skin infection, or, in rare cases, a growth requiring further evaluation.

Swollen lymph nodes on one side of the neck can have several causes. One possible reason is an infection, which can lead to swelling in the submandibular glands. These are located under the jaw and can become swollen due to various infections, as discussed by Eleftheriadis et al.

Another potential cause is tick-borne lymphadenopathy (TIBOLA), a condition where a tick bite leads to swollen lymph nodes, as explained by Lakos. This condition can cause the lymph nodes to become sore and enlarged.

Additionally, unilateral tonsillar enlargement, where one tonsil is larger than the other, can also cause swelling of the lymph nodes on one side of the neck. This is explored in the study by Cortez et al.

To summarize, infections, tick bites, and tonsillar issues are some reasons for swollen lymph nodes on one side of the neck.

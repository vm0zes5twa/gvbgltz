# Watch This 73-Year-Old Bodybuilder Go Flex for Flex With Terry Crews

The audition phase of any season of America's Got Talent is guaranteed to serve up two things at least once: an inspiring backstory, and a talent that you did not expect from somebody's outward appearance. In 73-year-old Josefina Monasterio, viewers get both.

[Flex for Flex](https://regeneration-desk.hbsnzj.com/)

Josefina grew up in a small town in Venezuela before moving to the United States, where she worked hard as a schoolteacher, and started a family. While she has always put her responsibilities in life first, Josefina recalls how she always loved to be in front of an audience, and believes she is a natural born entertainer.

"Now, in this later chapter of my life, I'm pursuing my dreams," she tells the camera ahead of her audition. "I want to show the world what I can do."

What she can do, it turns out, is pump iron. Josefina takes to the stage in a sparkly bikini which shows off her oiled muscles in true bodybuilder fashion, prompting the judging panel to speculate that she is decades younger than her actual age.

[This Year-Old Bodybuilder Go](https://mendstride.snaketongueofficial.com/)

"I started bodybuilding when I was 59 years old," Josefina tells judge Sofia Vergara, before launching into her act, striking a series of classic, Arnold Schwarzenegger-style bodybuilder poses to 'Pump It' by the Black Eyed Peas. The crowd instantly loves her, as she makes her way down into the audience and interacts with the judging panel, all the while repeating what is sure to become her catchphrase: "Pow!"

But the cheering intensifies when Josefina is joined on stage by America's Got Talent host and former Men's Health cover star Terry Crews, who sheds his shirt to show off his famously rippling physique and do some posing alongside the septuagenarian. Crews performs his trademark party trick, making his pecs dance in time to the music, before engaging in a flex-off with Josefina — where she ably keeps up, earning a bow of respect from Crews.

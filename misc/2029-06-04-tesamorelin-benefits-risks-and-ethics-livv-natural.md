Understanding Tesamorelin

Tesamorelin is a peptide comprising 44 amino acids. It’s a synthetic form of a human growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) analog. An extra trans-3-hexenoic acid group makes it last longer and remain more stable than GHRH.

Human growth hormone (HGH) is an essential part of our bodies with various purposes. It stimulates bone, cartilage, and muscle growth and increases protein production. Our brain’s anterior pituitary gland produces it naturally.

Tesamorelin may release HGH by binding to growth hormone-releasing hormone receptors. They stimulate the somatotropic cells, causing synthetic production of the hormone. These actions increase its presence in the body.

The Canadian biopharmaceutical company Theratechnologies Inc. developed tesamorelin to treat lipodystrophy. This condition causes an abnormal distribution or loss of fat tissue. People can inherit it genetically or acquire it as a result of another disease.

Lipodystrophy is common among HIV/AIDS patients. It usually results from receiving antiretroviral therapy for extended periods. Excess abdominal fat is one of the symptoms of the condition, and tesamorelin can help treat it.

The US Food & Drug Administration (FDA) made tesamorelin more famous in 2010. It approved the peptide for use in treating HIV-related lipodystrophy. Patients can get the brand name version, Egrifta, with a prescription. It’s currently unavailable to people who don’t have lipodystrophy.

Tesamorelin has shown promise in decreasing muscle fat in HIV-affected adults. It could increase muscle area simultaneously. People suffering from abdominal obesity may also benefit from it.

Scientists are researching tesamorelin further. Some believe it could promote fat loss in healthy individuals. Studies are exploring it as a treatment for nerve injuries and cognitive impairment.

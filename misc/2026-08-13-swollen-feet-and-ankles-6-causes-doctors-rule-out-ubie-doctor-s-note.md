Swollen Feet and Ankles: 6 Causes Doctors Rule Out

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Swollen feet and ankles are often caused by harmless factors like prolonged standing or salty foods. However, doctors systematically rule out six serious causes—deep vein thrombosis (DVT), heart failure, kidney disease, liver disease, venous insufficiency, and lymphedema—to catch potentially life-threatening conditions early.

Key symptoms, diagnostic tests, and warning signs should be evaluated before dismissing swelling as minor. Explanation

Swollen feet and ankles are common complaints. Often, swelling (edema) is harmless—caused by standing too long, salty foods or minor injuries. But sometimes it signals a more serious health issue. Doctors follow a checklist of potential causes to "rule out" anything that could be harmful. Understanding these six conditions can help you know when to seek medical advice without overreacting.

1. Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)

A blood clot in a deep leg vein can restrict blood flow, leading to sudden, painful swelling in one leg.

Key signs and tests:

Pain or cramping in the calf, often worse when walking

Mild swelling with fatigue or breathlessness deserves prompt attention. Lifestyle changes, medications and close monitoring can keep heart failure under control.

3. Kidney Disease

The kidneys regulate fluid and salt balance. When they're not working well, fluid can accumulate in the ankles.

Key clues:

Puffy eyelids in the morning

Reduced urine output or changes in urine color

High blood pressure

Blood tests for creatinine and blood urea nitrogen (BUN)

Urinalysis to check for protein or blood

Early kidney dysfunction often has few symptoms. Regular checkups and blood work can catch problems before significant swelling develops.

4. Liver Disease (Cirrhosis)

Scarred liver tissue can disrupt blood flow, leading to fluid buildup—often in the abdomen and legs.

What to watch for:

Swelling in feet and ankles alongside a distended belly (ascites)

Yellowish skin or eyes (jaundice)

Easy bruising or bleeding

Alcohol misuse, viral hepatitis or fatty liver disease can cause cirrhosis. Early diagnosis may slow progression and reduce complications.

5. Venous Insufficiency

Veins in the legs have one-way valves that push blood back to the heart. If valves weaken, blood pools, causing chronic swelling.

Common signs:

Heavy or achy legs after standing

Varicose (bulging) veins

Skin discoloration around the ankles

Leg cramps and itching

Duplex ultrasound to evaluate vein structure

Compression stockings often help

Simple lifestyle changes—leg elevation, exercise, weight management—can ease venous insufficiency and reduce swelling.

6. Lymphedema

Lymph vessels drain excess fluid from tissues. Damage or blockage leads to localized swelling, often in the feet or ankles.

Features to note:

Gradual swelling, sometimes limited to one leg

A feeling of tightness or heaviness

Thickening of the skin over time

Lymphoscintigraphy to map lymph flow

Physical therapy (manual lymph drainage) and compression wraps

Lymphedema can follow surgery, radiation therapy or infections. Early treatment prevents skin changes and progression.

When to Seek Medical Advice

Most mild swelling improves with rest, leg elevation and cutting back on salty foods. However, contact a doctor if you experience:

Sudden or severe swelling in one leg

Pain, redness or warmth in the calf (possible DVT)

Shortness of breath, chest pain or rapid heartbeat

Swelling with significant weight gain

Persistent changes in kidney or liver tests

Fever or signs of infection near the swollen area

These symptoms could indicate a life-threatening issue requiring immediate attention.

Take-Home Points

Swollen feet and ankles are usually benign but can signal serious conditions.

Doctors systematically rule out causes like DVT, heart failure, kidney or liver disease, venous insufficiency and lymphedema.

Home measures—leg elevation, compression, cutting salt—help mild edema.

See a doctor if swelling is sudden, painful, one-sided or comes with other concerning symptoms.

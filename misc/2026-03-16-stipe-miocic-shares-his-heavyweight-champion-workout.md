# Stipe Miocic Shares His Heavyweight Champion Workout

UFC heavyweight champ Stipe Miocic is coming off one of the biggest wins of his career. Back in August, Miocic took the final, decisive fight in a best of three series with Daniel Cormier at UFC 252—a win that cemented the Cleveland, Ohio native's status as one of the best heavyweights of all time for many UFC fans.

But Miocic would not be where he is today without his strength and conditioning coach Bobby Kaleal. Kaleal has created a full-body training program that focuses on getting Miocic fight-ready while focusing on injury prevention.

"With this whole workout, it's all endurance training. But also for stability, for strength for making things that are weak in my body stronger," says Miocic. "It only takes about 35 minutes, but we get everything. It hits every angle. We just don't lift, we get our money's worth in these 35 minutes, and it's definitely helped my career."

And his ultimate training goal is to get one percent better everyday.

"Don't ever get frustrated with your workouts. Just everyday, try to get one percent better. That's my mindset. Go at your own pace, and once you get past that pace, you can go bigger. Take your time and always have fun," says Miocic.

Stretching

"Everyday we go into a different type of stretching or dynamic warmup," says Kaleal. "So today we want to work on opening up his psoas and the front of his body, moving it in different directions. The more that we can open up his body and release some of the tension allows for more freedom and flexibility so he can perform at the optimal level."

Kaleal uses a resistance band with Miocic, holding a variety of stretches for 10 to 12 reps each.

He finishes with dynamic stretching and mobility to increase circulation.

The Warmup

Exercise 1: Band-Resisted Shadow Work6 to 8 minutes

"We want to get the mind right and stimulate that neuromuscular system, get the rust off the bolts so to speak," says Kaleal. "We start off slow, you go 2 to 3 minutes. Then he'll take the bands off, open up a little bit and get his feet and arms moving a little bit more. Then we'll reattach the bands and get into it more, digging in. And we'll send by taking them off and doing some motor speed work and hand work. Overall, 6 to 8 minutes."

Exercise 2: Eye-Hand Coordination Drills2 sets of 20 reps

For this, Miocic uses a ball that he throws against a wall while balancing on one leg for some ankle mobility work.

"The goal is to try to react as fast as possible, keeping that ball right in his face. And at the same time, you'll notice he's on one leg the whole time for some ankle stabilization work," says Kaleal.

From there, he will stand sideways and focus on peripheral vision while on one leg.

Exercise 3: Knee Stabilization Work2 to 3 sets of 20 to 30 seconds per foot

Using a band around his knees, Miocic does a number of knee stabilization moves.

"For Stipe what we feel it does is it creates confidence within the mind because he's had some issues with his knees in the past. So we give some good knee stabilization work where I'm moving and pulling in different directions, and he's just gotta work fast feet so that he's able to react in a fight like that," says Kaleal.

The Lifts

Exercise 1: Belt Squat Resisted Deadlift Squat Combo3 reps belt squat, 3 reps deadlift squat for 5 minutes each

"The goal is to re-emphasize and re-stimulate that neuromuscular system, and then go into some high kicks to loosen up the body that was just under tension, and then go into overspeed work to work his feet so that they can get up and get ready to go," says Kaleal. "So when you're in a fight situation, the mind and body understand what to do."

Exercise 2: Plyo Swing/March Sequence5 minutes

"The goal here is to constantly move your feet in different positions for 30 seconds, and then march your feet, put your knees up high, and have that one leg stabilize as he's pushing back," says Kaleal. "Think of rebounding a lot. This allows him to become more dynamic, more elastic in his movement, more explosive with deeper movement. It's not his favorite."

Adds Micoic, "It's great for stabilization. It's definitely helped my knees out a lot more. I've had some knee surgeries throughout my career, so this is one of my favorite ones. Even though I hate it the most, it's definitely the most beneficial."

Exercise 3: Inversion Curl5 minutes

"This is in my opinion by far the greatest apparatus or machine designed for the posterior chain," says Kaleal. "It really creates the strength in your hamstring based on the load you put on it. The stronger you get, the less weight you have on the machine because you're able to support more of your body weight."

Exercise 4: Band-Resisted Ab Work5 minutes

"Putting a big fat monster band on here and creating an increase in the resistance over time builds massive core strength with a lot of rotation and movement," says Kaleal. "You are constantly under tension for the 5 minutes. It's tougher than you think."

"Five minutes is not fun, not fun at all," says Miocic.

Exercise 5: Pullups5 minutes total

"This one I do 3 to 5 pullups followed by 8 to 10 seconds of shadowboxing," says Miocic. "The reason for this is I want to build the endurance for my muscles and staying strong, but also staying fluid with my punches. With the pullups, I make sure to go all the way down and all the way up and almost hang for a split second."

Exercise 6: Reverse Hypers5 minutes

"This is one of my favorite ones. I've always had lower back issues, and it definitely helps me strengthen it up," says Miocic.

Adds Kaleal, "The main thing is focusing on that posterior chain, focusing on your glutes and your low back and that connection there."

# This Obesity Medicine Doctor Weighed More Than 300 Pounds. These Strategies Helped Him Lose 125.

[More Than](https://collagen-atelier.bbsxyxy.com/)

Kevin Gendreau, MD, 38, of Fall River, Massachusetts admits he didn’t practice what he preached to his patients. Despite knowing the health risks associated with obesity, he had a habit of stress eating and mindless snacking in between a hectic job schedule. At his heaviest, he weighed up to 306 pounds. The loss of his beloved sister inspired him to get healthy.

[Weighed More](https://repair-cascade.jcesqc8.com/blog/7635880654.html)

MY WEIGHT GAIN happened slowly over the years. I dealt with alot of stress after my father was diagnosed with terminal melanoma in my freshman year of college. When he passed away 18 months later, I had gained 50 pounds. My meals were oversized, processed, and carb-heavy: sandwiches, cereal, crackers, chips, and cookies.

After college, I went to medical school, followed by residency. Like many doctors, I worked long, exhausting days. It caused me to gravitate towards more processed and sugary foods for comfort without even realizing it. I wasn’t eating fast food every night. Rather, I snacked mindlessly during long shifts. I would grab some food even when I wasn’t hungry. Most of college, medical school, and residency felt like "survival mode" for me. I wasn’t thriving, just surviving.

At one point, I reached 306 pounds. I was insulin-resistant and diagnosed with Type 2 diabetes, sleep apnea, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, and fatty liver disease at age 27. It was ironic. I’d become a family medicine physician in primary care counseling patients about the health risks of obesity. Yet here I was, dealing with all of these issues myself. I felt ashamed, overwhelmed, and stuck.

Then came the moment when everything changed. My sister Rachel was diagnosed with ovarian cancer. She was only 32 years old and the mother of two young children, Sophia and Henry. When my sister’s prognosis became terminal, I realized I needed to show up for her kids. Physically, emotionally, in every possible way. Her passing forced me to confront my life choices.

My weight was not healthy and, most of all, I wasn’t living the life I wanted. This mental clarity made me decide I needed to do something now. Tomorrow wasn’t guaranteed. I would work to lose the weight, not for myself but so my family do not have to worry about my health as well.

The Diet Plan to Reclaim His Health

I used my medical knowledge to create a plan that would help me lose weight and improve my existing health issues. I started with nutrition, switching my diet to low-carbs and whole foods. I focused on eating protein, healthy fats, and high fiber vegetables and berries.

[Helped Him Lose](https://innate-signal.jcesqc8.com/)

I paired this approach with time-restricted eating, also known as intermittent fasting. My schedule was (and still is) 2-3 low carb/low sugar/high protein meals eaten in a 6-hour window, from 12pm-6pm, then a fast until noon the next day. This helped to lower my blood sugar spikes and dial down my insulin resistance. It also allowed my body to tap into stored fat consistently.

I prioritized protein, fat, and fiber while minimizing processed carbohydrates and refined sugar. A typical meal became grilled chicken, sirloin steak or fish, and roasted non-starchy vegetables. I also ate avocado, nuts, olive oil, eggs, and cheese. Rather than counting calories, I took a mindful approach. Rather than finishing everything in front of me, I simply stopped eating when I felt full. It worked like a charm!

The Exercise Plan to Reclaim My Health

I committed to walking 10,000 to 15,000 steps a day. I tried to squeeze in walking whenever I could. I began walking after dinner or with my co-workers on my lunch break. I intentionally parked far away from my workplace, paced around the house, and took the stairs instead of the elevator. I hiked on the weekends, and jogged lightly. The activity cleared my head, and gave me a sense of control when life would get overwhelming.

When life got in the way or I was feeling discouraged, I stayed motivated by focusing on my "why." I wanted to be strong for my sister’s kids. I wanted to be an example to my patients. I wanted to reverse my diabetes, get my blood pressure under control, and come off the CPAP machine I used to manage sleep apnea. Most of all, I wanted to feel like myself again. Finding purpose in my diet and exercise was more powerful than any short-term motivation to lose weight for looks.

The Physical and Mental Transformation Results

I lost 125 pounds in 18 months. I went from 306 pounds to 181 pounds, in 18 months. It was steady, controlled weight loss of about 1 to 2 pounds per week. Today, I feel stronger and healthier than I ever did in my twenties. My Type 2 diabetes, fatty liver disease, sleep apnea, high blood pressure, and high cholesterol are all in remission. Emotionally, I feel aligned again. The confidence, energy, and clarity I gained transformed every part of my life.

I learned a lot about what it means to be healthy. Progress and consistency always trump perfection. Food quality matters more than quantity. Ultra processed carbohydrates and refined sugars were driving my hunger, inflammation, and constant cravings. Movement does not need to be extreme to be effective.

I also learned that you cannot hate yourself to make a transformation. Real and lasting change comes from the belief that you deserve to feel better. Most importantly, I realized that lifestyle changes are the single most powerful therapy we have for weight loss and improving long term metabolic health. My confidence skyrocketed. It wasn’t because I was thinner, but because I proved to myself that I could change my life.

Losing Weight Changed My Medical Career

My career path changed entirely. I became board-certified in obesity medicine in 2020, and I’ve dedicated my work to helping over 3,000 patients transform their health too. My weight loss journey also allowed me to show up more fully in my relationship with my husband, my family, and my community. I became more open, more joyful, and more present.

I lost the weight right before GLP-1 weight loss injections were made available to the public. Although I lost weight without medication, I never want people to feel judged for using tools like GLP-1 agonists. These medications can be lifesaving for the right patient. Bodies are different, biology is different, and people deserve access to whatever approach gives them the best chance at success.

My goal now is maintenance with meaning. I want to stay healthy for my family, to continue learning and growing, and to keep inspiring and motivating my patients as a physician.

I want to keep advocating for compassionate, evidence-based obesity care. I’m also expanding my work in education, clinical leadership, and writing. I’m trying to help reshape how we talk about metabolic health and weight bias in medicine in my clinic and on social media.

If I could give readers one piece of advice, it would be to start small. Focus on one change at a time, and allow your body to adapt. Consistent, small wins create the strongest momentum. Above all else, find your why. When your motivation is rooted in something deeper than a number on the scale, you become remarkably resilient. What I know now is that weight loss is not a test of willpower. It’s a complex process that involves biology, environment, and healing. Once you understand that, the shame lifts, and real progress becomes possible.

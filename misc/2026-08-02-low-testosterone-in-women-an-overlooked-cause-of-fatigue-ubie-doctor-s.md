Low testosterone in women is a frequently overlooked cause of persistent fatigue. While testosterone is often considered a male hormone, women also need it to support energy levels, muscle strength, mood, libido, and bone health. When levels drop below the optimal range, ongoing tiredness can result.

Common causes of low testosterone in women include:

Menopausal or perimenopausal hormonal decline

Ovarian dysfunction or surgical removal of the ovaries

Adrenal insufficiency

Certain medications, including oral contraceptives and corticosteroids

Chronic illnesses such as autoimmune conditions

Recognizing low testosterone alongside diet, sleep, and stress factors can help you and your doctor identify the right next steps.

Explanation

Fatigue is one of the most common complaints in women of all ages. While diet, sleep habits, stress and other hormones usually come under the microscope, low testosterone in women is often overlooked. Testosterone, though typically thought of as a "male" hormone, plays an important role in women's energy levels, mood, muscle mass and overall well-being. Recognizing how low testosterone can contribute to fatigue may help you and your doctor identify the right solution.

Why Testosterone Matters in Women

Testosterone is produced in small amounts by the ovaries and adrenal glands. In women, it contributes to:

Energy and stamina – Supports mitochondrial function and overall vitality

Muscle strength and tone – Helps maintain lean mass and efficient metabolism

Mood and motivation – Influences neurotransmitters like dopamine

Bone density – Works with estrogen to keep bones strong

Libido – Plays a key role in sexual desire and satisfaction

What to Expect from Treatment

Once low testosterone is confirmed, a tailored approach may include:

Balanced diet rich in healthy fats (avocado, nuts, olive oil) for hormone production

Consistent, moderate exercise combining strength training and cardio

Proper sleep hygiene to optimize natural hormone cycles

Mindfulness, meditation or yoga to reduce cortisol and support adrenal health

Counseling or coaching for emotional stressors

DHEA (under medical supervision) as a precursor to testosterone

Vitamin D, magnesium and zinc to support hormone synthesis

Low-dose testosterone patches, gels or injections (off-label in many countries)

Careful monitoring of blood levels and side effects

Each woman's situation is unique. Work closely with your healthcare provider to balance benefits and risks. Regular follow-up appointments and blood tests ensure you stay within optimal ranges.

When to Speak to a Doctor

If you experience any of the following, seek medical advice right away:

Chest pain, shortness of breath or palpitations

Severe depression or thoughts of self-harm

Sudden, unexplained weight gain or loss

Loss of consciousness or fainting spells

For non-urgent but persistent fatigue and associated symptoms, book an appointment to:

Review your detailed symptom history

Order relevant lab tests (including testosterone)

Discuss lifestyle, nutritional and therapeutic options

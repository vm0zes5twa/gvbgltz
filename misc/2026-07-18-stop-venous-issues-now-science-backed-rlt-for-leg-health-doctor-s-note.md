Chronic venous insufficiency (CVI), varicose veins and stasis dermatitis affect millions worldwide. Symptoms like swollen ankles, aching legs and skin changes can interfere with daily life. Understanding Chronic Venous Insufficiency

CVI occurs when the one-way valves in leg veins weaken or fail. Blood pools in the lower legs instead of returning smoothly to the heart. This leads to:

Leg heaviness, aching or cramping

Swelling (edema), especially after long periods of standing

Varicose veins (twisted, bulging veins visible under the skin)

Skin changes: dryness, darkening or itchiness (stasis dermatitis)

In severe cases, venous ulcers (open sores)

Left untreated, CVI can worsen over time, increasing risk of infection and reducing quality of life.

Traditional Treatments and Their Limits

Standard CVI therapies include:

Compression stockings. Support veins and improve blood flow, but can be uncomfortable in hot weather.

Exercise and leg elevation. Improve muscle pump function, but require consistent effort.

Medications. Diuretics or vein-strengthening agents may relieve symptoms but don't fix underlying valve issues.

Minimally invasive procedures. Laser ablation, sclerotherapy or surgery close off or remove diseased veins; effective but involve recovery time and costs.

While these approaches help many, residual symptoms or recurrence are common. That's why some people seek adjunct therapies such as red light therapy.

What Is Red Light Therapy?

Red light therapy uses low-level red or near-infrared (NIR) wavelengths—typically between 600–900 nm—that penetrate skin and tissue. Key mechanisms include:

Photobiomodulation: Mitochondria absorb light photons, boosting adenosine triphosphate (ATP) production and cellular energy.

Nitric oxide release: Light triggers release of nitric oxide, which dilates blood vessels and enhances microcirculation.

Anti-inflammatory effects: RLT reduces pro-inflammatory markers, easing swelling and discomfort.

Collagen synthesis: Stimulates fibroblasts to produce collagen, improving skin health and wound healing.

Because it's non-invasive and painless, red light therapy is used for skin rejuvenation, muscle recovery—and now, growing evidence suggests benefits for venous issues.

Clinical studies exploring red light therapy for chronic venous insufficiency and varicose veins are still emerging, but initial results are promising:

Improved microcirculation: A 2018 trial in the Journal of Photochemistry and Photobiology found that patients receiving NIR therapy showed a significant increase in skin blood flow over affected areas versus controls.

Reduced edema and pain: In a small pilot study, RLT applied three times weekly for four weeks led to measurable reductions in leg swelling and reported leg pain intensity.

Enhanced wound healing: For venous ulcers, combining standard wound care with RLT accelerated closure rates by up to 30% compared to wound care alone (Clinical Journal of Wound Care, 2017).

Anti-inflammatory action: Laboratory studies demonstrate downregulation of inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, TNF-α) in venous endothelial cells exposed to red/NIR light.

While larger randomized controlled trials are needed, these findings support red light therapy as a valuable adjunct to conventional CVI management.

Integrating RLT into Comprehensive Leg Care

Red light therapy works best when paired with proven venous-health strategies:

Compression garments: Wear as prescribed to support venous return.

Exercise: Calf-raise exercises and walking strengthen the calf muscle pump.

Weight management: Reducing excess weight lowers pressure on leg veins.

Skin care: Keep skin moisturized to prevent dryness and cracks that can lead to stasis dermatitis.

Regular check-ups: Monitor any new or worsening symptoms.

When to Seek Medical Advice

Although RLT is generally safe, it's not a replacement for medical evaluation. Speak to a healthcare professional if you experience:

Rapidly worsening swelling or pain

Redness, warmth or tenderness suggesting deep vein thrombosis

Open sores that won't heal

Shortness of breath or chest pain (possible pulmonary embolism)

Understanding Hormone Normal Ranges

Standard laboratory “normal” ranges are based on statistical averages from large populations, not on what is ideal for each person’s physiology. Hormone levels fluctuate throughout life, and individuals may feel unwell even when their results fall within the reference interval. [2]

For example, thyroid hormones such as TSH, T3, and T4 play a critical role in metabolism, energy, and cognitive function. Normal TSH levels typically fall between 0.4 and 4.0 mU/L, but some individuals experience symptoms of low thyroid health even within this range. [3]

Similarly, testosterone levels vary widely among men, and symptoms can appear even when levels are technically normal but on the lower end of the spectrum.

Other hormones—including cortisol, estrogen, and progesterone—also have broad reference ranges, and slight deviations from an individual’s optimal level can contribute to low energy, mood swings, sleep issues, and metabolic changes.

Symptoms That May Indicate Suboptimal Hormone Function

Even when hormone levels fall within the “normal” range, symptoms may indicate that the body is not functioning optimally. Hormones act as chemical messengers, and even small shifts can create noticeable changes in how you feel. [4]

Common symptoms of subtle hormone imbalance include:

Persistent fatigue and low energy, even with adequate sleep

Brain fog and difficulty concentrating, often linked to thyroid health or cortisol fluctuations

Mood swings, anxiety, or depression, which can occur when estrogen, progesterone, thyroid hormones, or testosterone are not balanced

Poor sleep quality, often tied to cortisol dysregulation

Low libido or sexual dysfunction, commonly associated with testosterone or estrogen imbalance

Sitting too much is strongly linked to obesity. When you spend long hours seated, your body burns fewer calories, muscle activity slows, and fat—particularly around the abdomen—tends to accumulate. Prolonged sitting also reduces overall physical activity, which further contributes to weight gain and related health risks like heart disease and type 2 diabetes.

Explanation

A sedentary lifestyle, which means sitting or lying down a lot, is closely linked to obesity. Recent studies suggest that when people sit for long periods, they tend to exercise less. This lack of movement can lead to weight gain, especially around the belly, which is called abdominal obesity.

Less Movement: When people sit too much, they don't burn as many calories. This can make it easier to gain weight.

Abdominal Obesity: This is when extra weight gathers around the belly. It can be more dangerous than fat in other areas because it is linked to health problems like heart disease.

Overall Health: Being active is important for staying healthy. Even small changes, like standing up more often, can help reduce the risk of obesity.

By understanding how sitting too much affects our bodies, we can make better choices to stay healthy.

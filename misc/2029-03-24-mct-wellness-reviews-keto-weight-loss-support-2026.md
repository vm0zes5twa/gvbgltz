Safety

Gundry MD utilizes third-party testing and manufactures its products in GMP-certified facilities in the United States (similar to competitors Garden of Life and Naomi). And although the specific quantity of MCT in MCT Wellness is unclear due to its proprietary blend, even if the entire 5.5g of the blend was MCT oil, it’d still be significantly lower than the potentially dangerous upper limit suggested by some researchers (anywhere from 50-100g).8

When it comes to allergens, Gundry MD is not alone in using coconut oil as the source of its MCT oil. If you have an allergy to coconuts (or other tree nuts), it’s best to steer clear of any MCT oil product that uses oils derived from them. Additionally, one study performed on mice revealed evidence that MCT oils can potentially worsen peanut allergies and anaphylactic reactions.13

Efficacy

MCT Wellness may be effective for some people, but the ingredient amounts (even obscured) aren’t equivalent to those in studies where benefits were seen. For example, one study that showed improvements in the cognition of patients with Alzheimer’s used 42g of MCT per day.9 And a separate study examining the benefits of MCT oil on weight loss and metabolic factors gave doses of 18g to women and 24g to men.10

Additionally, the supplement’s MitoHeal (Redcurrant and Blackcurrant Extract) and CogniGrape (one ingredient in MCT Wellness’ Grape Polyphenol Complex) suffer from the same underdosing issue. One study on the effectiveness of MitoHeal on subjects’ skin health used 500mg and 1,000mg, while MCT Wellness only offers 400mg.11 And while some promising research on CogniGrape’s effects on cognition does appear to use the same amount that’s in MCT Wellness (250mg), it’s worth mentioning that Gundry MD uses CogniGrape as part of a blend — it’s not just 250mg of pure CogniGrape.12 Like the product’s MCT blend, the actual amounts are unknown.

While it’s not uncommon for companies to use proprietary blends in supplements, we wish Gundry MD was more transparent about the ingredients in MCT Wellness. There are ways to add transparency without divulging the exact amounts. One competing brand, Naomi, does this by listing the approximate percentage of total MCTs in the blend used for its Creamy MCT Oil Powder.

It’s also important to note that Gundry MD’s claim that bioflavonoids help MCT or other ketones “travel through your body at light speed” has no current research or evidence to back it up.

Frequently asked questions about MCT oil

What is MCT?

MCT stands for “medium-chain triglycerides.” Triglycerides, as the “tri” in the name implies, are made up of three chains of fatty acids, which help energize your body. The medium size of MCTs makes it easier for your body to convert them into energy; this is in contrast to long-chain triglycerides (LCTs) — often found in soy, corn, safflower, and sunflower seed oils.5 30

Where does MCT oil come from?

MCT oil is most often derived from coconut oil, but it can also be found in palm kernel oil (the latter of which has been the source of environmental controversies, such as its production potentially being the cause of nearly half the deforestation in tropical rainforests).

What are the potential health benefits of MCT oil?

MCT oil may be able to assist in weight loss, increase energy levels, and enhance athletic performance. Additionally, some evidence shows that it may contribute to improvements in cognition, diabetes management, autism, inflammation, and Alzheimer's disease.5

What’s the best way to take MCT oil?

MCT oil is typically found in a liquid (oil) or powder form, though there are also some capsule options on the market. Currently, there’s no research supporting any variance between powders, liquid, or capsule forms of MCT oil in terms of effectiveness. The only difference we’ve seen reported throughout our research is that some users note that the powdered forms of MCT oil are less harsh on their stomachs. So, ultimately, whichever form of MCT oil fits your lifestyle best is the best way for you to take it.

Can you take MCT oil every day?

Research on the long-term effects of MCT oil is limited, with one of the few studies finding it safe after only monitoring MCT oil use for 16 weeks.10 But, it’s important to note that the study wasn’t focused on the long-term effects of MCT oil specifically; rather, it was about the effects of MCT oil versus olive oil on metabolic risk factors. And while the aforementioned study was conducted on humans, a mouse study from 2017 found that MCTs increased liver fat.31 However, the mice in the study were fed extremely high amounts of MCT oil, much higher than what you’d take if adhering to supplement directions. So, ultimately, MCT oil at the recommended amounts or dosages is likely safe to take daily, but do keep in mind that the long-term effects on humans haven’t been thoroughly researched.

What are the side effects of MCT oil?

Common side effects of MCT oil include stomach upset, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea. In some extreme or long-term use cases, MCT oil can potentially lead to fat buildup in the liver.5 31

Does MCT oil break a fast?

While MCT oil on its own is keto-friendly due to not containing any carbohydrates, a tablespoon of it amounts to around 115 calories. Whether you consider a single calorie or 50 calories (a widely-accepted limit) to be fast-breaking, a typical serving of MCT oil will probably break your fast.

Does MCT oil turn into ketones?

Medium-chain triglycerides are so good at turning into ketones that research has shown MCTs can potentially induce ketosis without fasting or eating a traditional keto diet. This is particularly true of a type of MCT called caprylic acid, also known as C8.17

What does MCT oil taste like?

MCT oil on its own has a “neutral or slightly bland” taste.32 Many MCT oil products are flavored, like MCT Wellness — which comes in Raspberry Medley or Watermelon Lemonade. Some brands even offer creamier styles of flavors, such as Naomi’s Creamy MCT Vanilla Coconut Swirl. Since MCT oil has a practically nonexistent flavor by default, companies across the market have taken advantage of this to produce a multitude of flavor options.

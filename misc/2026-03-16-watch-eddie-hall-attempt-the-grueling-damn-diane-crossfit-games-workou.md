# Watch Eddie Hall Attempt the Grueling ‘Damn Diane’ CrossFit Games Workout

As a globally famous strongman and former winner of the World's Strongest Man title, Eddie Hall is no slouch in the gym. But in a recent training session, Hall put his fitness to the test with a notoriously intense CrossFit WOD. Joined by CrossFit vlogger Craig Richey, Hall took on the "Damn Diane," a recent qualifier event in the CrossFit Games.

Subscribe to Men's Health

The workout consists of 15 deadlifts and 15 strict handstand pushups. Naturally, Hall kills the deadlifts; he set a world record for heaviest deadlift when he pulled 1,100 pounds back in 2016. Then, in order to not push his shoulder mobility, he does a wall walk to get into the handstand pushup position for the second portion of the workout, before completing 15 reps with strong form.

"Those handstands, they just take it out of you from top to bottom," he wheezes when he's done. "It's not the shoulders, it's a lot of core."

After the workout, Richey reiterates how awed he is by Hall's fitness when it comes to factors other than strength.

"Seeing him on the machines, obviously, with his weight, like yeah, he's going to be able to pull bigger because he can move all the weight, and on the bike he can do a bit more, but the numbers he was holding, I was like, 'shit, he's doing the same as me,' for the duration of the whole thing, and then on the runner, he could do the whole runner, and then for the workout, he could do 4 and a half minutes," he says. "Yeah, the deadlift weight wasn't that heavy, but the capacity to be able to do the strict handstand pushups, I was impressed. The guy is fit."

For Hall, though, this is all part and parcel of his ongoing mission to lean down and work on his speed, agility and explosiveness as he prepares for his grudge match with rival strongman Hafthor Bjornsson. "You don't train 2 or 3 hours of cardio each day to not be fit, so it's paying off," he says. "The boxing training is paying off, it's nice to come and hang out with an elite athlete and do alright."

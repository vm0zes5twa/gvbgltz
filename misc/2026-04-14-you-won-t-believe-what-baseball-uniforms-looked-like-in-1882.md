1882: Baseball Players Wear Uniforms Based On Their Position

These days, everyone on the same team wears the same uniform, save for each player’s individual name and number. But that wasn’t always the case.

Back in 1882, uniforms were based on position rather than team.

First basemen, for instance, wore the same scarlet-and-white-striped shirt no matter which team they were on. As you’ve probably noticed, the strategy didn’t quite take off.

It lasted just a year before people started calling the uniforms “clown costumes” and ditched the concept.

At the start of the 1929 season, the Cleveland Indians and the New York Yankees took the field wearing numbers on the back of their jerseys.

Most teams agreed and quickly followed suit—except for the Philadelphia Athletics, who held out until 1937.

1939: Batting Helmets Debut

It seems like a common-sense safety practice now, but players didn’t begin wearing helmets in the batter’s box until 1939.

It took more than a decade for MLB to officially make helmets a requirement.

1951: Numbers Are Added To the Front Of Jerseys

Twenty-two years after numbers debuted on the backs of jerseys, teams figured it made sense to add them to the front, too.

The Springfield Cubs were the first to try it, and the Brooklyn Dodgers followed suit in 1952.

1960: Names Are Added To the Backs Of Jerseys

The Chicago White Sox started adding names to their uniforms, and all the other teams adopted this practice—except for the Yankees, who continue to leave names off to this day.

Though the Yanks have never released a formal statement on why, some historians say it’s because giving attention to an individual player undermines the rest of the team.

Doctor's Note: Saving Your Edges – Protective Styles and Nutrients for Women on GLP-1s

Many women taking GLP-1 medications (such as semaglutide or liraglutide) for weight management or diabetes notice changes in their hair—especially thinning around the hairline, often called "edges." While not everyone experiences this side effect, rapid weight loss and shifts in nutrition can trigger temporary hair shedding (telogen effluvium). The good news? With the right protective styles, gentle care and key nutrients, you can support healthy hair growth and preserve your edges.

Understanding GLP-1 and Hair Edge Preservation

GLP-1 receptor agonists work by increasing insulin release, slowing gastric emptying and reducing appetite. Commonly prescribed brands include:

Semaglutide (Ozempic®, Wegovy®)

Liraglutide (Victoza®, Saxenda®)

Dulaglutide (Trulicity®)

While these medications are effective for glycemic control and weight loss, the rapid change in calorie intake and body fat can push more hair into the resting phase, leading to temporary shedding. Proper hair care and nutrition can help mitigate this effect.

Protective Hair Styles to Preserve Edges

Maintaining low-tension, low-manipulation hairstyles minimizes stress on fragile edges. Consider these protective style principles:

Gentle Tension• Avoid tight ponytails, high buns or styles that pull directly on your hairline.• Opt for loose buns, low ponytails or clipped-back styles.

Low-Manipulation Options• Braids and twists: Keep them loose, don't braid flush to the scalp.• Wigs and extensions: Use a silk or satin cap beneath to reduce friction.• Headscarves and turbans: Soft, breathable fabrics (silk, satin) protect hair.

Limit Heat and Chemicals• Air-dry whenever possible. If you must blow-dry, use a cool or low-heat setting.• Delay chemical treatments (relaxers, perms, harsh dyes) until hair regains strength.

Nighttime Care• Sleep on a silk or satin pillowcase to reduce friction.• Wrap edges in a silk scarf or use a silk bonnet.

Key Nutrients for Hair Edge Health

Dietary changes or reduced food intake on GLP-1s may lead to suboptimal levels of vitamins and minerals vital for hair growth. Consider these nutrients:

Protein• Hair is ~90% protein (keratin). Aim for 0.8–1.0 g/kg body weight daily.• Choose lean meats, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy, beans, lentils and tofu.

Iron• Iron deficiency is linked to hair shedding.• Sources: red meat, poultry, seafood, beans, spinach, fortified cereals.• Consider a supplement only after checking your ferritin and hemoglobin levels with your doctor.

Zinc• Supports hair follicle health and repair.• Sources: oysters, beef, pumpkin seeds, chickpeas, cashews.

Biotin (Vitamin B7)• Plays a role in keratin production.• Found in eggs, almonds, sweet potatoes, spinach.• Deficiency is rare but supplementing 2,500–5,000 mcg/day may help, under medical guidance.

Vitamin D• Low levels are associated with increased hair shedding.• Sun exposure and foods like fatty fish, fortified milk and mushrooms help maintain levels.• Your doctor can check 25-hydroxyvitamin D to determine if you need a supplement.

Omega-3 Fatty Acids• Anti-inflammatory properties may support scalp health.• Sources: cold-water fish (salmon, mackerel), flaxseeds, chia seeds, walnuts.

Daily Hair Care Routine

Consistency is key. A gentle regimen protects new growth and preserves edges:

Shampoo & Condition• Use a sulfate-free, gentle shampoo 1–2 times per week.• Follow with a lightweight, hydrating conditioner.

Scalp Massage• 1–2 times weekly, massage scalp for 2–5 minutes to boost circulation.• Use your fingertips or a soft-bristle scalp brush.

Leave-In Treatments• Apply a light, water-based leave-in conditioner or natural oil (jojoba, grapeseed) to the scalp and edges.• Avoid heavy petroleum-based products that can clog follicles.

Trimming• Trim split ends every 8–12 weeks to prevent breakage from traveling up the strand.

Lifestyle Tips for Optimal Results

Healthy habits support both your weight-loss goals and hair health:

Balanced Meals• Focus on whole foods: vegetables, fruits, lean proteins, whole grains, healthy fats.• Small, frequent meals can help maintain nutrient intake if appetite is low.

Hydration• Aim for at least 8 cups of water per day, more if exercising.

Stress Management• Chronic stress can worsen hair shedding.• Practice mindfulness, deep breathing or yoga.

Sleep• Aim for 7–9 hours nightly to support cellular repair and growth.

When to Seek Professional Advice

Temporary hair shedding often resolves within 3–6 months once your body adjusts to weight change and new eating patterns. However, consult your healthcare provider if you notice:

Rapid, excessive hair loss beyond general thinning

Bald patches or scarring at the hairline

Signs of infection on your scalp (redness, itching, severe pain)

Other concerning symptoms (dizziness, fatigue, chest pain)

Final Thoughts

Preserving your edges while on GLP-1 therapy boils down to gentle styling, targeted nutrition and healthy habits. Remember:

Protect edges with low-tension styles and silk accessories

Prioritize protein, iron, zinc, biotin and vitamin D

Maintain a gentle but consistent hair-care routine

Stay hydrated, manage stress and get enough sleep

If you have any life-threatening or serious symptoms, please speak to a doctor right away. For any persistent hair loss or nutritional concerns, schedule a consultation with your healthcare provider. Your edges—and your overall well-being—are worth it.

Internal Medicine

Vomiting blood (hematemesis) is often a sign of bleeding somewhere in the upper digestive tract, most commonly from a stomach ulcer, a tear in the esophagus (Mallory-Weiss tear), inflammation (gastritis or esophagitis), or ruptured blood vessels. The blood may appear bright red or resemble dark coffee grounds, depending on the source and how long it has been in the stomach.

Hematemesis is considered a medical emergency, especially when paired with warning signs such as dizziness, rapid heartbeat, black or tarry stools, severe abdominal pain, shortness of breath, or fainting. These symptoms can indicate significant blood loss that requires immediate treatment.

Because vomiting blood can stem from many conditions—ranging from mild irritation to life-threatening internal bleeding—understanding the possible cause is critical. Vomiting blood means you may have bleeding in your upper digestive system, such as in your stomach or esophagus. This condition is called hematemesis.

One common cause is a small tear, sometimes from intense vomiting, which is known as a Mallory-Weiss tear. Other possible reasons are stomach ulcers or blood vessel problems (like esophageal varices) that can bleed more seriously. While sometimes a minor tear might clear up on its own, other causes represent more serious conditions.

Look for warning signs like dizziness, feeling very weak, a rapid heartbeat, or if the blood looks bright red or like coffee grounds. These symptoms can indicate a significant loss of blood or ongoing bleeding and should result in you calling emergency services immediately.

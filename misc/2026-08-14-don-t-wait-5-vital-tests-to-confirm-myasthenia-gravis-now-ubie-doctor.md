What tests are used to confirm a diagnosis of myasthenia gravis?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Myasthenia gravis is confirmed through several key diagnostic tests: blood tests to detect specific antibodies (such as acetylcholine receptor or MuSK antibodies), nerve conduction studies and electromyography (EMG) to measure muscle response, and imaging tests like a CT or MRI scan to examine the thymus gland. Doctors may also perform an edrophonium test or ice pack test to assess muscle weakness patterns.

Because myasthenia gravis symptoms often overlap with other neurological and autoimmune conditions, getting clarity early is important. To confirm if someone has myasthenia gravis, doctors do several tests:

Blood Tests: These look for antibodies in your blood that can cause myasthenia gravis. The most common ones are called acetylcholine receptor antibodies and anti-MuSK antibodies.

Nerve Tests: One important test is called electromyography (EMG). It checks how well your nerves and muscles talk to each other. Another test is called repetitive nerve stimulation (RNS), which helps see if your muscles get tired quickly.

Imaging Tests: Sometimes, doctors use a CT scan or MRI to check your chest. They look for a problem with a gland called the thymus, which can be involved in myasthenia gravis.

These tests help doctors understand if myasthenia gravis is causing muscle weakness and how to best treat it. Each test gives different information that helps doctors make a diagnosis.

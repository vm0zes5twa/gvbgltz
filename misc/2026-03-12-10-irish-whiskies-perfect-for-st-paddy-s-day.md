# 10 Irish Whiskies Perfect For St. Paddy's Day

When you think of whiskey, the scotch varieties probably come to mind first. But, at one point, Irish whiskey reigned supreme before all the distilleries on the Emerald Isle shut down thanks to things like Prohibition, trade wars, economic uncertainty, and more. Now, though, that’s all changing.

Dubbed “the comeback kid” amongst its biggest advocates (bartenders and master distillers), the spirit is making its way back to the top, apparent with the slew of new releases from big players and little guys alike, plus the reopening of many distilling spots (looking at you, Tullamore D.E.W. and Jameson).

For St. Patrick’s Day, it only makes sense to pop open a bottle and cheers not only to the holiday but also to Irish whiskey’s resurgence. Here are the ones we’ll be sipping on the holiday (and beyond!).

Knappogue Castle 14-Year Old Twin Wood

This 2016 Irish Whiskey of the Year award winner consists of not one, but two 14 year single malts. They’re each aged in separates casks, one a bourbon barrel and the other an Oloroso sherry cask, which brings light hints of fruitier flavors like green apple and grapefruit zest. Notes of vanilla from the charred oak casks make themselves known to your palette, too, delivering a lighter, warmer taste.

The Tyrconnell 15 Year Old Madeira Cask Finish Single Malt

[Perfect For St](https://mindpeptide.bbsxyxy.com/)

One of the oldest Irish whiskey-producing company introduces its latest and most aged expression. The limited release double distillation is made of just two ingredients—fine Irish barely and pure Irish spring water. Aged in American White Oak ex-bourbon barrels then finished in Madeira wine casks from Portugal, it has rich flavors with a complex taste and finish. It first starts out soft then surprises your tastebuds with a burst of spice before drying down to a fresh punch and lingering with a hint of zest.

Teeling Single Grain

This single grain expression stands out from the crowd with its focus exclusively on corn, whereas other whiskies only incorporate one corn element. That and the fact that it’s only one of a handful of single grain bottlings in the world sets the sweet yet wine-like spirit apart. It fully matures in cabernet sauvignon casks from California, imparting a berry-like taste balanced by the corn. It’s then bottled at 46 proof with no chill filtration so you never lose any of the flavor.

Slane Irish Whiskey

We’re not surprised that this crowd pleaser made its way back onto another one of our lists—are you? Co-founder and director Alex Conyngham says to enjoy it on St. Patrick’s Day, start with an Irish coffee. “Use strong freshly ground coffee, Demerara sugar, Slane Irish Whiskey and then floated with lightly shaken heavy cream, topped off with a grating of dark chocolate.” Once nighttime rolls around, Conyngham likes to sip it on the rocks. The smooth triple cask distillation is appropriate for all kinds of celebrations, though, or even a night spent home alone. It delights all palates—experienced whiskey taster or not—with flavors like caramel and butterscotch (almost rich like toffee) and surprises all noses with a sweet spice aroma.

The Sexton Single Malt

Ornate and opulent, this single malt’s bottle makes it look way more expensive than it actually is (you can find it for under $30). Not only does it look good on your home bar but the spirit inside is as impressive as one that’s of a higher price point. Hand-selected casks crafted of toasted European oak, and seasoned with Oloroso sherry house the whiskey as it matures giving it both dried fruity and oaky flavors. That combined with the fact that it’s crafted with Irish malted barley and triple-distilled in copper pots makes for a smooth finish every time.

Kilbeggan Single Grain

[For St Paddy's Day](https://incretin-path.escortskhi.com/)

Aged for an undisclosed number of years in bourbon barrels, this whiskey truly absorbs the flavors of the cask it’s sat in. Finished in a marriage of different bourbon and various wine barrels, the single grain spirit—meaning it’s made in a single location of malted barley plus one other grain—gives it a smooth flavor thanks to impurities and bitter notes removed during the distillation process. You can enjoy it neat or in a cocktail.

2 Gingers Blended

We’re selecting this (relatively) new kid on the block not just because of its sleek, modern bottle but also because it perfectly blends smooth, malty, and sweet flavors for the ultimate sip. 2 Gingers has the taste of a whiskey made by centuries of master distillers, belying the fact that the brand is just seven years old. The liquor itself has been aged four years in the famed Kilbeggan Distillery in Ireland. With a woody base complemented by a tingle of honey and citrus, plus a caramel and vanilla finish, it’s a perfectly balanced blend that can be appreciated by all.

Tullamore D.E.W. Rum Cask Finish

[St Paddy's](https://weight-signal.sunsimiaosd.com/)

Mixing two of our favorite spirits, this triple-distilled blend of pot still, malt, and grain Irish whiskey is finished in XO rum casks, adding sweet flavors and warm spices that evokes a tropical vacation every time you imbibe. It pays homage to the Irish immigrants who were involved in the development of rum in the Caribbean back in the 17th century in more ways than one—the label design is inspired by trade maps, passports, and stamps. In order to fully appreciate the distinct, deep notes of caramel and banana with hints of dates and raisins (which some other whiskies might have but this one puts the forefront), it must be sipped slowly and savored.

Bushmills Red Bush

Bushmills is Ireland’s oldest licensed distillery, so they’ve been around way before we started celebrating St. Patrick’s Day. This well-rounded red label concoction has been triple-distilled and aged in bourbon casks for an extra smooth finish. Belfast natives Sean Muldoon and Jack McGarry of The Dead Rabbit (the Irish pub in New York City for the modern man that was voted World’s Best Bar in 2016) have partnered with the brand for the occasion offering tips and tricks on how to keep it real Irish on St. Patrick’s Day: sipping Red Bush “over one large ice cube.”

[For St Paddy's](https://benpepsi.com/blog/6302421317.html)

Jameson Caskmates IPA Edition

Is it a really an Irish whiskey roundup without featuring the brand that probably first introduced you to the spirit? This new edition to the Caskmates collection is more complex and also appropriately festive for the holiday with its very green bottle. Perhaps you remember the brand’s first Caskmates release, which was finished in stout barrels? This one was matured in IPA beer-soaked barrels adding crisp hoppy notes. The same signature Jameson smoothness is still there but complemented by citrusy notes.

[St Paddy's Day](https://insulin-pathways.hbsnzj.com/blog/7536097382.html)

Method and Madness Single Pot Still

[Whiskies Perfect](https://copperglow.dns0663.com/blog/4394994977.html)

This unique bottle may draw a double take, since its design is non-traditional, but embrace the aesthetic. As part of the Midleton’s new range of distillations—single grain, single malt, and a 31 year old single grain are also part of the collection—it’s a new take on a single pot still, which is uniquely Irish. Matured in American and sherry barrels but finished in French chestnut. The result is a mix of sweet, spice, and everything nice.

Who Can Administer Therapy

Besides ownership regulations, state regulations might also address who can administer IV therapy. In California, registered nurses can administer IV therapy under the supervision of a physician, physician assistant, or nurse practitioner. In New York, an LPN must be directly supervised by a physician, physician assistant, nurse practitioner, or registered nurse. Some states may also allow emergency medical technicians or paramedics to administer IV therapy in specific settings.

Supervision and Documentation

Supervision and documentation requirements can also differ by state. The Corporate Practice of Medicine

The corporate practice of medicine (CPOM) is a legal doctrine that typically prohibits corporations, entities, or non-physicians from practicing medicine. In most states, piercing the outermost layer of the skin or the epidermis and administering fluids and vitamins via a compound is considered practicing medicine and is subject to state CPOM regulations. The impact of CPOM on IV therapy businesses can differ significantly across states. Other states, such as Florida, permit physicians and non-physicians to own a medical entity but require hiring a physician as the medical director to maintain the physician-patient relationship. Proper Diagnoses and Treatment

In addition to adhering to CPOM and state regulations, proper diagnoses and treatment are crucial in IV therapy. Since IV therapy is a medical procedure, it’s essential to ensure that patients receive appropriate care in line with their diagnoses and treatment needs. Before providing any services, a good-faith exam or initial evaluation must be conducted to determine if the patient is suitable for IV therapy. A physician, physician assistant, or nurse practitioner can perform the good-faith exam in most states. Following the exam, the provider may prescribe a course of treatment and delegate the treatment’s administration to a non-physician provider. Non-physician providers can include physician assistants, nurse practitioners, registered nurses, or, in some states, licensed practical nurses or licensed vocational nurses. It’s important to note that only a few states permit the administration of IV fluids by individuals other than those mentioned above, such as emergency medical technicians or paramedics. In addition to proper administration, documenting the treatment plan and any adverse reactions to the treatment is essential. This documentation involves maintaining accurate medical records that include the patient’s medical history, treatment plan, and any adverse reactions experienced.

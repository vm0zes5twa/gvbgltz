Irregular periods often indicate a hormonal imbalance that can ripple across your overall health. When your cycle is unpredictable, it may reflect how your body regulates energy, insulin, and reproductive hormones. In some cases, irregular periods are an early sign of conditions like polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), thyroid disorders, or metabolic issues. Left unaddressed, these imbalances can increase the risk of long-term concerns such as infertility, cardiovascular disease, and type 2 diabetes.

Explanation

When your periods are irregular, it means that the cycle between one period and the next might be too short, too long, or unpredictable. This can happen because of differences in the hormones that control your body. Sometimes, irregular periods mean that your body may not use food the right way, and this can lead to problems with body weight or blood sugar. In some cases, women with irregular periods have a condition called polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). PCOS can make it harder to have a baby, and can sometimes cause extra hair or acne. Some research also shows a link between long or irregular menstrual cycles and a slight increase in the risk of ovarian cancer. Even if you feel fine, paying attention to changes in your period can help you and your doctor watch for problems early. If you notice your cycle is often irregular, you can use Ubie's free AI symptom checker to help identify potential causes and determine whether you should see a healthcare professional.
